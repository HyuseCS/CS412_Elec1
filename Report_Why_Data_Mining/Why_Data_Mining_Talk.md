# Why Data Mining - 5 Minute Talk

Source: Han, Kamber and Pei, *Data Mining: Concepts and Techniques*, Chapter 1.

## Slide 1: Too Much Data, Too Little Information (0:00-1:30)

- The book calls it "the explosive growth of data"
- We live in the "information age", also called the "data age"
- People cannot read it all: "data rich but information poor"
- Unused data becomes "data tombs"

**Script:**
Good day, everyone. My topic today is "Why Data Mining". It comes from Chapter 1 of the book by Han, Kamber and Pei. I want to answer one simple question. Why do we need data mining at all?

Let us start with the problem. The book says we live in the information age. Some people call it the data age. The book uses a strong phrase: "the explosive growth of data". Data is not growing slowly. It is growing very fast. Businesses record every sale. Scientists record every experiment. Hospitals record every patient. Our phones and web searches create data all day. Computers and cheap storage made this possible.

But having data is not the same as having answers. The book says we are "data rich but information poor". We have a lot of data, but little useful information. No person can read millions of records. Our old tools can store data and search it. They cannot tell us what the data means. The book says that data archives can become "data tombs". These are places where data goes in, but nobody looks at it again. That is a waste, because useful patterns may be hidden inside.

## Slide 2: Data Mining Turns Data into Knowledge (1:30-3:00)

- Turn raw data into useful knowledge
- Also called knowledge discovery from data (KDD)
- Like finding gold in rock or sand

**Script:**
So what is the answer? Data mining. Data mining is the process of finding interesting patterns and useful knowledge in large amounts of data. It turns raw data into knowledge that people can use.

The book says a better name might be "knowledge mining from data". Many people also use the term KDD. This means knowledge discovery from data. KDD is the whole path. We collect and clean the data. Then we find the patterns. Then we check them and show them in a way people can understand. Data mining is the key step in that path.

The book gives a nice picture. Think about mining gold from rock or sand. You do not want the rock. You want the gold. In the same way, we do not want the raw data. We want the knowledge inside it. A person cannot dig through that much data by hand. A computer can search it fast, and it can find patterns that a person would never see. That is why data mining matters. It makes our data useful, and it saves the data from becoming a tomb.

## Slide 3: Three Examples and Closing (3:00-4:45)

- Stores: market basket analysis
- Banks: credit card fraud detection
- Hospitals: patient risk prediction

**Script:**
Let me give three examples.

First, stores. A shop can look at what customers buy together. This is called market basket analysis. For example, the shop may find that some items often sell together. Then it can place them close on the shelf, or offer a good deal on both. This helps the shop, and it helps the customer find what they need.

Second, banks. A bank can study a large number of card payments. It learns what normal spending looks like for each customer. When a payment looks very strange, the system can flag it as possible fraud. The bank can then check it fast. This protects the customer and the bank. No person could watch every payment, but a data mining system can.

Third, hospitals. A hospital can study past patient records. It can find which patients may be at higher risk of a serious problem. Then doctors and nurses can act early, and give extra care to the patients who need it most.

In all three cases, the data was already there. The store had its sales. The bank had its payments. The hospital had its records. Data mining made that data useful.

Let me finish. Data is growing very fast. People cannot read all of it, so we become data rich but information poor. Data mining solves this. It turns raw data into useful knowledge. That is why we need it. Thank you for listening. I am happy to take any questions.
